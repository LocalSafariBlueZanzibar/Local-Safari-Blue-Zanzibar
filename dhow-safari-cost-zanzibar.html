Zanzibar Dhow Safari Cost Guide 2026 — Prices, Inclusions & How to Save

Updated October 2026 7 min read Authentic Dhow Expeditions

One of the first questions travelers ask us on WhatsApp is a simple one: "How much does a dhow safari in Zanzibar actually cost?"

The honest answer is that it depends on how you book. The same day — same dhow, same reefs, same lunch — can cost very different amounts depending on where you click "book".

This guide explains why, in plain language, and shows you how to get the best value for the exact same experience.

Note: This guide is written by Authentic Dhow Expeditions, a locally-owned dhow safari operator based in Fumba Village, Zanzibar. Prices mentioned are based on our own observations of the Zanzibar market in 2026. Our own tour is priced at $85 per person.

The Short Answer

Typical dhow safari prices in Zanzibar in 2026 range from $60 to $150 per person. The low end is almost always direct booking with a local crew; the high end is usually a hotel desk, agent, or international tour platform. The actual tour is broadly the same — the reef, the sandbank, Kwale Island, and a seafood lunch.

What Drives the Price Up or Down?

Three things explain almost every price difference you will see:

💰 Agent commissions

Hotels, tour desks, and international platforms add a markup on top of the operator's price.

⛵ Boat size & group size

Larger boats cost more per trip to run, and typically carry more guests onboard.

🎫 Inclusions

Pickup, park fees, gear, drinks, and lunch are sometimes excluded — and added later.

What Is Usually Included at Each Price Level

Budget ($70–$90)

  • Full-day dhow safari
  • Snorkeling stop at coral reef
  • Sandbank stop
  • Kwale Island visit
  • Seafood BBQ lunch
  • Snorkeling gear and life jackets
  • Soft drinks and water

Mid-Range ($90–$130)

  • Everything above, plus:
  • Hotel pickup and drop-off
  • Slightly bigger boat
  • Guaranteed shade area

Premium ($120–$150+)

  • Everything above, plus:
  • Guaranteed seat on a "quality" boat
  • Potential for a private guide
  • Often a longer itinerary
Important: The actual marine experience — the reef, the sandbank, Kwale Island, the food — is essentially identical at every price level. The only meaningful differences are group size, pickup, and how much of your money goes to an agent.

Hidden Costs to Watch For

Some operators advertise a low base price but add these extras later. Always confirm in writing before you book:

  • Marine park / conservation fees — usually $5–$10 per person (some operators include this).
  • Hotel pickup — $20–$40 depending on where you are staying.
  • Snorkeling gear — some operators charge $5–$10 if you do not bring your own.
  • Drinks beyond water — beer and soft drinks can be extra.
  • Gratuities — customary but never mandatory.

How to Save Money Without Sacrificing Quality

Here is the honest truth: the best value in Zanzibar comes from booking direct with a local, registered operator. Not a hotel desk. Not a random agent. Direct.

Here is why:

  • You skip the middleman commission. That is usually a meaningful portion of the price.
  • You get a real person on WhatsApp. Ask questions, get answers, no guessing.
  • You usually get a smaller group. Many local operators run small-group boats.
  • Your money stays local. It goes to the crew and the Fumba community, not a corporate chain.

Booking direct does not mean "risky" — it means direct. Any legitimate local operator will provide their BPRA registration number on request. Ours is Z0000313934.

What About Tipping?

Tipping is not obligatory, but it is appreciated. As a rough guide:

  • $5–$10 per couple — for excellent service
  • $10–$20 — for larger groups or a full private charter
  • Optional — always optional, never pressured

Tips are usually shared among the crew — the captain, the cook, and the guides. If you want to tip a specific person, hand it to them directly.

Payment: Cash or Card?

Most local operators prefer cash on the day of the tour — either USD, EUR, or TZS. Some accept mobile money (M-Pesa, Tigo Pesa, Airtel Money). A few accept card payment through a link or terminal.

At our own operation:

  • Cash — USD, EUR, or TZS on arrival
  • Mobile money — accepted on request
  • Card — available on request
  • No prepayment required — you pay on the day

Is It Worth Paying More for the "Premium" Option?

Honest answer: usually not, unless you value a specific feature.

Paying more does not get you a "better reef" — the reef is a public conservation area, and no operator has private access. It does not get you "better fish" — everyone buys from the same market in Fumba. It rarely even gets you a "better boat" — the dhows are all traditional wooden boats.

What it can get you:

  • Hotel pickup included
  • A larger boat with more shade
  • Guaranteed booking during peak season
  • The convenience of booking through your hotel desk

Those are real conveniences. Just know what you are paying for.

The Bottom Line

You can enjoy the same beautiful day in Menai Bay — the reefs, the sandbank, Kwale Island, and the seafood feast — for a wide range of prices per person.

If you want the most value, book directly with a registered local operator. If you prefer the convenience of your hotel desk or a big platform, book there — and know you are paying for the convenience.

Either way, the reef is waiting.
