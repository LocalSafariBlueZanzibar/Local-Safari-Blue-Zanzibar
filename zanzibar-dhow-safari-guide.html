Zanzibar Dhow Safari Guide — How to Choose the Right Operator

Updated January 2026 6 min read Authentic Dhow Expeditions

If you are planning a trip to Zanzibar, a day on a traditional wooden dhow sailing through Menai Bay is almost certainly on your list. It is one of the island's most iconic experiences — and for good reason: the turquoise water, the coral reefs, the sandbank, and the fresh seafood lunch are unforgettable.

But when it comes time to book, the options can feel overwhelming. Large international operators, mid-size local companies, and small family-run crews from Fumba Village all offer something that sounds similar. Prices range from $60 to $150. Some tours have 80 guests; others have eight.

This guide explains, in plain language, what a dhow safari actually includes, why the names and prices vary so much, and how to choose the operator that matches what you actually want.

What Is a Dhow Safari in Zanzibar?

A dhow safari is a full-day boat trip on a traditional wooden sailing vessel — the same style of boat that Zanzibari fishermen have used for centuries. Nearly all dhow safaris depart from Fumba Village, on the southwest coast of Unguja island, and sail into the protected Menai Bay Conservation Area.

The classic itinerary includes:

  • Sailing on a traditional wooden dhow
  • Snorkeling at coral reefs inside Menai Bay
  • A stop at a natural white sandbank (sometimes called the "Blue Lagoon")
  • A visit to Kwale Island, with its baobab tree and small beach
  • A seafood BBQ lunch with fresh fish, lobster, calamari, and tropical fruits
  • Optional hotel pickup and drop-off from anywhere in Zanzibar

Because the entire bay is a marine conservation area, all operators — large and small — follow the same route and stop at the same protected reefs. No operator has a "secret spot."

Why Prices and Group Sizes Vary So Much

If the itinerary is broadly the same, why do some tours cost $60 and others $150? Why do some take 80 guests and others take 10?

There are three main reasons:

  1. Agent commissions. If you book through a hotel reception, a tour desk, or an international travel platform, that middleman takes 20–40% of the price. Operators that sell direct can offer the same day for less.
  2. Boat size. Larger boats carry more guests but cost more per trip to run. Smaller dhows carry fewer guests, which lowers overhead — and creates a completely different experience.
  3. Operating model. Some companies are structured as international businesses. Others are small family crews who live in the village they sail from. Their cost bases are very different.

None of these differences are inherently good or bad. They simply mean you should know what you are paying for.

About the Famous "Safari Blue" Name

Many travelers researching Zanzibar dhow safaris encounter the name Safari Blue early on. It refers to a well-established tour company that has operated from the Fumba area for many years, and it has become one of the most recognizable names in Zanzibar tourism.

Because the name is so widely known, it is sometimes used informally in Zanzibar to describe the whole category of Fumba dhow trips — much like people in some countries say "Coke" for any cola. This means that when you ask a hotel or taxi driver to "book you a Safari Blue," you may actually be sent to any of several independent operators running a similar route.

That is not a problem in itself — but as a traveler, it helps to know the difference so you get exactly what you are looking for.

How to Book a Dhow Safari with Confidence

Whichever operator you choose, here is a simple checklist to make sure you are booking safely:

  1. Book direct when possible. Contact the operator on WhatsApp or through their website. Direct booking means no middleman markup and a real person answering your questions.
  2. Confirm the operator's registration. A legitimate business in Zanzibar will have a Business Registration (BPRA) number and can share it on request.
  3. Ask about group size. A genuine small-group operator will tell you exactly how many guests will be on your boat.
  4. Confirm what is included. Snorkeling gear, life jackets, lunch, drinks, and any park fees should be clearly stated. Nothing should be a surprise.
  5. Check recent reviews. Look for reviews from the past 3–6 months on Google, TripAdvisor, or social media.
  6. Pay attention to communication. A good local operator answers quickly, answers clearly, and never pressures you into paying a large deposit up front.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is every dhow safari in Fumba the same route?

Yes — all operators sail inside the same Menai Bay Conservation Area and visit the same well-known reefs, sandbanks, and Kwale Island. The route is regulated by the conservation authority. What differs is the size of the group and the style of the day.

Can I book a dhow safari for less than the international tour platforms charge?

Almost always, yes. Booking directly with a local operator typically costs 20–40% less than booking through an agent or international platform, because you skip the commission. Our own all-inclusive tour is $85 per person, booked direct.

Do smaller operators use the same snorkeling spots and sandbanks?

Yes. All operators visit the same protected reefs and sandbanks within Menai Bay. The difference is that smaller groups are easier to manage in the water, and you get more attention from the crew.

How do I know an operator is legitimate?

Ask for their BPRA business registration number, look for recent real reviews, and always be able to speak with a real person on WhatsApp before paying anything. Legitimate operators never demand full prepayment before the day of the tour.

What is the best time of year for a dhow safari in Zanzibar?

The dry seasons — from June to October and from December to February — offer the calmest seas and clearest water. The long rains (April–May) can affect sea conditions, though many operators still run trips during the short rains (November).
