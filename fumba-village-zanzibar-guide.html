Fumba Village — The Real Home of Zanzibar's Dhow Tours

Updated October 2026 8 min read Authentic Dhow Expeditions

If you have booked a dhow safari in Zanzibar, you have probably seen the name Fumba Village in the itinerary. It is the departure point for almost every traditional dhow tour on the island — but most travelers never learn much about it.

Fumba is not just a harbor. It is a real Zanzibari fishing village with its own history, community, and daily rhythm. Understanding it makes your dhow safari more meaningful — and it gives you a glimpse of Zanzibar that most tourists miss.

This guide tells you everything you need to know about Fumba Village: where it is, why dhow tours start here, what life is like there, and how to visit respectfully.

Note: This guide is written by Authentic Dhow Expeditions, a locally-owned dhow operator based in Fumba Village. We have been operating from this village since 2010 — everything below comes from first-hand experience.

Where Is Fumba Village?

Fumba Village sits on the southwest coast of Unguja, the main island of Zanzibar. It is roughly 30 minutes by car from Stone Town, and about 45 minutes from Zanzibar International Airport.

The village overlooks the Menai Bay Conservation Area — the largest marine protected area in Zanzibar. Fumba is essentially the gateway to that bay: it sits at the northern edge, where the reef, sandbanks, and islands begin.

Why Do Dhow Tours Start From Fumba?

Fumba is not the only coastal village in Zanzibar — but it is the best starting point for dhow tours, for three reasons:

1. Geography

Fumba sits at the mouth of Menai Bay, which means dhow tours can reach the best reefs, sandbanks, and Kwale Island in less than 30 minutes of sailing. Other departure points would take an hour or more just to arrive at the same location.

2. Traditional Dhow Culture

Fumba has been a fishing village for generations. The local families have built and sailed traditional wooden dhows here for centuries. When you board a dhow in Fumba, you are not just joining a tour — you are stepping into a living maritime tradition.

3. The Menai Bay Conservation Area

Menai Bay is one of the most protected marine areas in Zanzibar. Fishing is regulated, anchoring is controlled, and the coral reefs have been allowed to recover and thrive. The bay is home to dolphins, sea turtles, and hundreds of species of reef fish.

Fun fact: Every dhow safari in Zanzibar — regardless of the operator — sails into the same protected Menai Bay Conservation Area. There is no "secret route." Fumba is simply the closest and best-located starting point.

What Is Life Like in Fumba Village?

Fumba is a quiet, working fishing village. Most residents are involved in one way or another with the sea — as fishermen, boat builders, cooks, or tour crew.

Here is what you would see on a walk through the village:

  • Wooden dhows lined up at the shoreline — some of them decades old, still in use
  • Fishermen returning in the morning with the night's catch
  • Children playing near the water, some already learning to sail
  • Women selling fresh fish, vegetables, and spices at the small local market
  • Boat builders repairing hulls, rigging sails, and stitching nets
  • Small shops and tea stalls where locals gather in the afternoon

It is not a tourist village. There are no resorts, no hawkers, and no gift shops. It is simply a Zanzibari community living the way its parents and grandparents did — with the addition of tourism that has developed around the dhow tours.

How Tourism Has Changed Fumba

Before dhow tours became popular, Fumba was a purely fishing-based economy. Today, tourism provides an additional source of income for many families — but it has been carefully managed.

The shift has been positive:

  • Families can now earn from tourism without leaving their village
  • Young people have job opportunities as captains, cooks, and guides
  • Traditional dhow-building skills are being preserved and passed on
  • The community has an economic incentive to protect the bay from overfishing

But the village still lives primarily from the sea. Dhow tours are an additional livelihood — not a replacement for fishing.

The Menai Bay Conservation Area

Menai Bay is the protected marine area that all dhow safaris from Fumba sail into. It was designated a conservation area in 1997, and it covers approximately 470 square kilometers of ocean.

What makes it special:

  • Dolphins — bottlenose and spinner dolphins are frequently spotted, especially in the early morning
  • Sea turtles — green and hawksbill turtles graze on the seagrass beds
  • Coral reefs — healthy and diverse, with hundreds of species of tropical fish
  • Mangrove forests — vital nursery habitats for fish and crustaceans
  • Kwale Island — the famous baobab-tree island at the heart of the bay

The conservation area is managed by the Zanzibar Department of Fisheries with support from local communities — including the people of Fumba Village.

How to Visit Fumba Village

There are two ways most travelers experience Fumba:

1. As Part of a Dhow Safari

This is the easiest and most common way. You arrive at Fumba harbor, meet your captain and crew, and board the dhow. Many operators offer hotel pickup from anywhere in Zanzibar.

2. As a Standalone Visit

You can also visit Fumba by taxi from Stone Town (about 30 minutes). There is no entrance fee, no ticket, and no formal attraction. It is simply a village — and a beautiful place to spend a quiet hour before or after a tour.

What to Know Before You Visit

Fumba is a conservative Muslim village. Visitors are welcome, but a few simple courtesies go a long way:

  • Dress respectfully — cover shoulders and knees when walking in the village
  • Ask before photographing people — especially women and children
  • Do not enter private homes without an invitation
  • Support local businesses — buy fruit, drinks, or snacks from village stalls
  • Be mindful of prayer times — the village observes the five daily prayers
  • Bring small change — TZS notes are useful for small purchases
Respectful traveler: The people of Fumba have welcomed visitors for years. A friendly "Jambo" and a genuine smile will open more doors than any tour booking.

Fumba vs Other Departure Points

Most Zanzibar dhow safaris depart from Fumba, but a few depart from other villages. Here is how Fumba compares:

  • Fumba (Southwest) — The classic departure point. Closest to Menai Bay, Kwale Island, and the Blue Lagoon. Our home base.
  • Nungwi / Kendwa (North) — Departure for Mnemba Atoll snorkeling trips. Longer sail time, different reef system.
  • Kizimkazi (South) — Departure for dolphin-watching tours. Focused on marine wildlife rather than sandbanks.
  • Stone Town (West) — Departure for Prison Island and sunset cruises.

If you want the classic Zanzibar dhow experience — snorkeling, sandbank, Kwale Island, seafood lunch — Fumba is the right departure point.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Fumba Village worth visiting outside of a tour?

Yes, if you appreciate real local life. It is not a tourist attraction in the traditional sense, but it is a beautiful, peaceful village with a strong maritime culture. Many travelers enjoy spending an hour there before or after a dhow tour.

Can I stay overnight in Fumba?

There are a few small guesthouses in and around Fumba, but most travelers stay in Stone Town or on the northern beaches and visit Fumba only for the dhow tour. We recommend staying where you are comfortable and letting us arrange pickup.

Is Fumba safe for tourists?

Yes. Fumba is a quiet, welcoming village with a very low crime rate. The local community is used to visitors, and the area around the harbor is well-patrolled by the tour operators themselves.

What language do people speak in Fumba?

Swahili is the first language. English is widely spoken by tour guides, captains, and younger residents. A few words of Swahili ("Jambo", "Asante") are always appreciated.

When is the best time to visit Fumba?

The dry seasons (June–October, December–February) offer the best weather and sea conditions. However, Fumba Village itself can be visited year-round — the village life continues regardless of the weather.

Can I book a dhow tour directly in Fumba?

Yes, but it is much easier to book in advance online or via WhatsApp. Booking direct with a registered local operator guarantees you a place on the boat and gives you time to ask questions before your trip.

Final Thoughts

Most travelers see Fumba only for a few minutes — arriving by car, walking to the harbor, and boarding a dhow. But Fumba is more than a departure point. It is a place with its own life, history, and rhythm.

If you have time before or after your dhow safari, take a few minutes to walk through the village. Buy a fresh coconut. Say hello to the fishermen. Watch the dhows come and go. You will understand Zanzibar a little better — and you will remember it longer.
