Safari Blue Zanzibar Explained — And How to Book a Local Alternative

Updated January 2026 6 min read Authentic Dhow Expeditions

If you are planning a trip to Zanzibar, you have almost certainly come across the phrase "Safari Blue". It is one of the most searched terms for things to do on the island, and for good reason: the tour it describes — a full day of sailing, snorkeling, sandbank lounging, and a seafood feast in Menai Bay — is genuinely one of the best experiences Zanzibar has to offer.

But there is a lot of confusion among travelers about what Safari Blue actually is, who runs it, and how to book it. In this honest guide, we explain everything clearly — including how to choose a local alternative if you want a smaller, more personal, and often more affordable version of the same day.

Disclosure: This guide is written by Authentic Dhow Expeditions, an independent locally-owned dhow operator based in Fumba Village, Zanzibar. We are not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to Safari Blue or any of its related companies. Any reference to Safari Blue is for factual description and comparison only.

What Is Safari Blue?

Safari Blue is the name of a well-known, long-established dhow tour company operating from the Fumba area in the southwest of Zanzibar. It is one of the largest and most recognized tour operators on the island, having run boat trips into the Menai Bay Conservation Area for many years.

The tour typically includes:

  • Sailing on a traditional wooden dhow
  • Snorkeling at coral reefs inside Menai Bay
  • A stop at a natural sandbank (sometimes called the "Blue Lagoon")
  • A visit to Kwale Island
  • A seafood BBQ lunch with fresh fish, lobster, calamari, and tropical fruits
  • Hotel pickup and drop-off (usually for an additional fee)

It is a fantastic experience, and thousands of travelers enjoy it every year.

Why Locals Call Almost Every Fumba Boat Trip "Safari Blue"

Here is the part most travel guides never explain — and it is important.

In the Fumba area, the phrase "Safari Blue" has become a household name. Much like people in some countries say "Coke" when they mean any cola, or "Kleenex" when they mean any tissue, travelers and even local boatmen often use the words "Safari Blue" to describe the entire category of dhow trips that leave from Fumba and visit Menai Bay.

This means two things:

  1. When you ask a hotel receptionist or a taxi driver in Zanzibar to "book you a Safari Blue," they may send you to any of several operators — not necessarily the company whose actual business name is Safari Blue.
  2. There are several locally-owned, smaller, independent operators from Fumba Village — including our own team — that run essentially the same itinerary, often with smaller groups, personal service, and lower prices.

This is not a trick. It is simply how language evolves around a famous brand. But as a traveler, you should know the difference so you get exactly what you want.

How to Book a Local Alternative to Safari Blue

If you decide a local operator is right for you, here is how to do it safely:

  1. Book direct. Contact the operator on WhatsApp or through their website. Direct booking means no middleman markup and a real person answering your questions.
  2. Confirm the operator's registration. In Zanzibar, a legitimate business will have a Business Registration (BPRA) number and can share it. Ours is Z0000313934.
  3. Ask about group size. A real small-group operator will tell you exactly how many guests will be on your boat.
  4. Confirm what is included. Snorkeling gear, life jackets, lunch, drinks, and park fees should be clearly stated. Nothing should be a surprise.
  5. Check recent reviews. Look for reviews from the past 3–6 months on Google, TripAdvisor, or social media.
  6. Pay attention to how they communicate. A good local operator answers quickly, answers clearly, and never pressures you.

What About the Real "Safari Blue" Company?

If you specifically want to book with the company whose legal business name is Safari Blue, that is completely fine. Contact them directly through their official website or their official Google Business Profile. Avoid booking through unofficial agents or "copycat" pages that use similar names — some are scams.

The important thing is to know which you are booking, and to make the choice on purpose rather than by accident.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Safari Blue the same as the tour to Kwale Island?

The tour that leaves Fumba, visits a sandbank, snorkels in Menai Bay, and stops at Kwale Island is often called "Safari Blue" by travelers — but the itinerary itself is not exclusive to any one company. Several local operators, including ours, run the same route.

Is Safari Blue worth it?

Yes — the day itself (the reefs, the sandbank, the seafood) is genuinely beautiful, no matter which operator you choose. What differs is the size of the group and the personal feel of the day.

Can I book Safari Blue cheaper?

If by "Safari Blue" you mean the general Fumba dhow experience, yes — booking directly with a local operator typically costs less than booking through a hotel desk or agent, because you skip commissions. Our own all-inclusive tour is $85 per person, booked direct.

Do local operators use the same snorkeling spots and sandbanks?

Yes. All operators in the Fumba area sail inside the same protected Menai Bay Conservation Area and stop at the same well-known reefs, sandbanks, and Kwale Island. The route is regulated by the conservation authority — no operator has a "secret" reef.

How do I know an operator is legitimate?

Ask for their BPRA business registration number, look for recent real reviews, and always be able to speak with a real person on WhatsApp before paying anything. Legitimate operators never demand full prepayment before the day of the tour.
