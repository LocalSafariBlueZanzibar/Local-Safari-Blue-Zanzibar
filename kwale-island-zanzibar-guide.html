Kwale Island — Complete Guide to Zanzibar's Legendary Baobab Island

Updated October 2026 7 min read Authentic Dhow Expeditions

If you have ever seen a photo of a dhow safari in Zanzibar, chances are you have seen Kwale Island without knowing its name. It is the small, palm-covered island where almost every Menai Bay boat tour stops for lunch — and where one of the oldest trees in the archipelago stands on the beach like a silent guardian.

This guide tells you everything you need to know about Kwale Island: what it is, what makes it special, and what to expect when you visit.

Note: This guide is written by Authentic Dhow Expeditions, a locally-owned dhow safari operator based in Fumba Village, Zanzibar. Kwale Island is inside the Menai Bay Conservation Area and is visited by all licensed operators.

Where Is Kwale Island?

Kwale Island sits in the middle of Menai Bay, off the southwest coast of Unguja (the main island of Zanzibar). It lies roughly 20 minutes by dhow from Fumba Village, which is where almost all Menai Bay safaris begin.

Because the bay is a designated Conservation Area, the island and the surrounding reefs are protected. That is why the water is so clear and the marine life so abundant compared to other parts of Zanzibar.

Why Is Kwale Island Famous?

Three things make Kwale Island special:

1. The Giant Baobab Tree

The most photographed feature of Kwale Island is a massive African baobab tree standing on the beach. Baobabs are sometimes called "the tree of life" because they store water in their enormous trunks and can survive droughts that kill other vegetation.

The Kwale baobab is estimated to be over 200 years old. Its trunk is enormous, and local guides love to tell guests that the tree was once used as a natural landmark by fishermen navigating the bay.

2. The Peaceful Beach

Unlike the busier beaches of Nungwi or Kendwa, Kwale Island's beach is quiet and untouched. The sand is fine and white, the water is warm and shallow, and there are no hawkers or beach vendors. It is the kind of place where you can genuinely relax.

3. The Seafood Lunch

Most dhow safaris stop at Kwale Island for lunch. Tables and benches are set up on the beach or under simple shade structures, and guests are served a freshly grilled seafood feast: fish, lobster, prawns, calamari, rice, and tropical fruits. The food is prepared by the crew, using ingredients brought from Fumba that morning.

Insider tip: Ask your captain to point out the baobab tree as soon as you land. It is only a 2-minute walk from the main lunch area, but many guests miss it because they are focused on the food.

What Can You Do on Kwale Island?

  • Swim in the shallow water just off the beach — perfect for children and non-swimmers.
  • Walk around the island — the full loop takes about 20–30 minutes at a slow pace.
  • Photograph the baobab tree — it is the island's most iconic feature.
  • Snorkel off the shore — small coral patches close to the beach have colorful fish.
  • Enjoy the seafood lunch served by your crew.
  • Watch the dhows sail in and out of the bay.
  • Just relax — many guests nap on the sand after lunch.

When to Visit Kwale Island

  • June to October: Best overall. Calm seas, clear water, comfortable temperature.
  • December to February: Very good. Warm water, mostly calm, busier with tourists.
  • March & November: Variable. Can be beautiful or rough, depending on the day.
  • April to May: Not recommended. Long rains, rough seas, many operators closed.

How to Get to Kwale Island

Kwale Island is reached only by boat. There are no ferries or public transport. Every visitor arrives on a dhow as part of a Menai Bay safari.

The typical route is:

  1. Depart from Fumba Village around 8:30–9:00 AM.
  2. Sail into Menai Bay, with a stop for snorkeling at the coral reefs.
  3. Stop at a natural sandbank (the "Blue Lagoon") for photos and swimming.
  4. Arrive at Kwale Island around midday.
  5. Seafood BBQ lunch on the beach, plus free time.
  6. Short walk to see the baobab tree.
  7. Sail back to Fumba, arriving around 4:00–4:30 PM.

Some operators combine the visit with dolphin watching (usually in the early morning) or a stop at a smaller nearby island.

What to Bring to Kwale Island

  • Swimwear (worn under your clothes)
  • Towel and dry clothes to change into
  • Sunscreen (reef-safe if possible)
  • Hat and sunglasses
  • Water shoes — helpful for the rocky shoreline
  • Waterproof bag for phone and camera
  • Cash for tips or drinks (though most tours are all-inclusive)

Is Kwale Island Suitable for Families?

Yes. In fact, Kwale Island is often the highlight for families with children. The beach is shallow, calm, and safe. Life jackets are provided on all tours. The only thing to watch for is sun exposure — the island has little natural shade, so bring a hat and plenty of sunscreen.

Is Kwale Island Worth It?

Absolutely. If you take a dhow safari in Menai Bay and skip Kwale Island, you have missed one of the most peaceful, photogenic, and unforgettable parts of the day. It is where the boat stops, the food appears, and the island quietly does the rest.

A Word About the Conservation Area

Kwale Island sits inside the Menai Bay Conservation Area, which means:

  • No littering is permitted — take everything back with you.
  • No touching or removing coral, shells, or marine life.
  • No anchoring on living coral — operators use designated mooring points.
  • Fishing is regulated and only licensed fishermen may fish.

All licensed operators in Fumba, including ours, follow these rules. If you see a tour operator breaking them, please report it — the bay is a shared treasure.

Final Thoughts

Kwale Island is not a large island, and there is not much on it. But that is exactly the point. It is a quiet place — sand, sea, a 200-year-old tree, and lunch on the beach. In a world where so many places are overdeveloped, Kwale remains what Zanzibar used to be.

We hope you enjoy it as much as we do.
