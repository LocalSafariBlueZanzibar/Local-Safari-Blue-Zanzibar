Local Tours in Zanzibar — Why Locals Do It Better

Updated October 2026 9 min read Authentic Dhow Expeditions

If you are planning a trip to Zanzibar, you will quickly notice that tour options fall into two broad categories: local operators and large corporate operators. Both can show you the same beautiful places. But the experience — and where your money goes — is very different.

This guide explains what a local tour in Zanzibar actually means, why it matters, and how to find and book genuine local operators.

Note: This guide is written by Authentic Dhow Expeditions, a locally-owned dhow operator based in Fumba Village, Zanzibar. We are a local tour operator ourselves, and everything below is written from our own perspective on the industry.

What Is a "Local Tour" in Zanzibar?

A local tour is a tour run by a company that is based in Zanzibar, owned by Zanzibaris, and staffed by Zanzibaris. The guides, the boat crew, the cooks, and the managers are all from the local community — often from the exact village you are departing from.

This is different from a corporate tour operator, which may be:

  • Registered outside Zanzibar (sometimes internationally)
  • Managed remotely
  • Staffed by a mix of local and foreign employees
  • Focused on maximising volume rather than experience

Both models exist in Zanzibar, and both are legal. But they produce very different days on the water.

Why Local Tours Are Different

Smaller Groups

Local operators usually run small groups — often under 15 guests per boat. Corporate operators often fill much larger boats.

Personal Attention

With fewer guests, the crew can speak with everyone, answer questions, and adjust the day to the group.

Deep Local Knowledge

Local captains grew up on these waters. They know where the fish are, where the wind shifts, and where to find the quietest spots.

Money Stays Local

When you book with a local operator, your money goes to the crew, the village, and the community — not to a corporate head office.

Better Prices

No agent or platform commissions. Local operators can offer the same day for lower prices, without cutting corners on safety.

Environmental Care

Local operators have a long-term interest in protecting the reef and the bay — because they live here.

How to Spot a Genuine Local Operator

Not every operator that calls itself "local" actually is. Here is how to check:

  1. Ask for their BPRA registration number. All legitimate operators in Zanzibar have one. Ours is Z0000313934.
  2. Check where they are based. A real local operator will have a physical base — often in a village like Fumba, Nungwi, or Kizimkazi.
  3. Look at how they communicate. A genuine local operator replies directly, quickly, and personally on WhatsApp — not through an automated system.
  4. Check the reviews. Real guests, real names, real dates.
  5. Ask about the crew. The crew should be from the local community, not a rotating foreign staff.
  6. Confirm you can pay on arrival. Local operators usually do not demand full prepayment.

Why Booking Local Matters

When you book with a local operator, you are not just buying a tour. You are choosing:

  • Direct support for a Zanzibari family — instead of an international platform
  • Employment for local captains and crew — who live in the village you depart from
  • Preservation of local knowledge — passed down for generations
  • Long-term protection of the reef — because the reef is their livelihood
  • A more personal, more authentic day — the kind travelers remember
Small choice, big impact: The difference between booking direct with a local and booking through an agent is often a $20–$40 difference in price — and a very different day on the water.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are local tours in Zanzibar safe?

Yes. Local operators in Zanzibar are regulated by the Zanzibar Commission for Tourism and must comply with maritime safety standards. Life jackets, first aid, and trained crews are standard on licensed tours. Ask for the BPRA number to confirm legitimacy.

Are local tours cheaper?

Almost always yes. Booking direct with a local operator means no agent or platform commissions, so the same day on the same boat can cost significantly less.

Do local operators speak English?

Yes. English is widely spoken by tour guides and captains in Zanzibar, especially in tourism areas. Many guides also speak Italian, French, or German.

Can I book a local tour last minute?

In low season, yes — often the same day. In high season (June–October, December–February), it is best to book 3–5 days ahead.

How do I pay a local operator?

Most local operators accept cash on the day of the tour (USD, EUR, or TZS). Some accept mobile money (M-Pesa, Tigo Pesa, Airtel Money) or card on request. Prepayment is usually not required.

What if I have a problem with a local tour?

Speak with the operator directly first — most issues are solved on the spot. If you cannot resolve it, the Zanzibar Commission for Tourism handles complaints.

Are local tours suitable for families and children?

Absolutely. Local operators often specialise in small, family-friendly tours. Life jackets are provided for all ages, and the pace is usually more relaxed than corporate group tours.
